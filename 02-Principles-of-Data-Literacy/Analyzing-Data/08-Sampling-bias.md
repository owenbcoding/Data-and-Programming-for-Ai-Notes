# Sampling Bias

## Understanding Sampling Bias
Sampling bias occurs when a sample is not representative of the population it's intended to represent, leading to systematic errors in conclusions.

## Types of Sampling Bias

### Selection Bias
- **Definition**: Systematic differences between selected and non-selected
- **Example**: Surveying only people who respond
- **Impact**: Unrepresentative sample
- **Detection**: Compare sample to population characteristics
- **Solution**: Improve sampling methods

### Non-Response Bias
- **Definition**: Non-respondents differ from respondents
- **Example**: Busy people don't respond to surveys
- **Impact**: Biased sample
- **Detection**: Compare early vs. late respondents
- **Solution**: Follow-up with non-respondents

### Self-Selection Bias
- **Definition**: People choose whether to participate
- **Example**: Product reviews only from motivated customers
- **Impact**: Biased toward extremes
- **Detection**: Compare to external benchmarks
- **Solution**: Random sampling

### Survivorship Bias
- **Definition**: Focus on survivors, ignoring failures
- **Example**: Analyzing only successful companies
- **Impact**: Overestimates success rates
- **Detection**: Consider missing cases
- **Solution**: Include all cases

### Coverage Bias
- **Definition**: Sample doesn't cover entire population
- **Example**: Landline telephone surveys miss cell-only users
- **Impact**: Biased sample
- **Detection**: Compare to population demographics
- **Solution**: Improve coverage

## Sampling Methods and Bias

### Probability Sampling
- **Simple random**: Every member has equal chance
- **Systematic**: Select every kth member
- **Stratified**: Sample from each stratum
- **Cluster**: Sample clusters
- **Bias**: Generally lower bias when done correctly

### Non-Probability Sampling
- **Convenience**: Easy to access members
- **Judgment**: Researcher selects members
- **Quota**: Specific numbers from groups
- **Snowball**: Referrals from participants
- **Bias**: Generally higher bias

## Common Mistakes

### Assuming Random Sampling
- **Problem**: Assuming convenience sample is random
- **Impact**: Ignoring bias
- **Solution**: Understand sampling method

### Ignoring Non-Response
- **Problem**: Not accounting for non-response
- **Impact**: Biased results
- **Solution**: Analyze and adjust for non-response

### Over-Generalizing
- **Problem**: Generalizing beyond population
- **Impact**: Invalid conclusions
- **Solution**: Be clear about population

### Inadequate Sample Size
- **Problem**: Sample too small
- **Impact**: High sampling error
- **Solution**: Adequate sample size
